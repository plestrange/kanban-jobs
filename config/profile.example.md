# Profile

Written against `<your-resume-file>` (`<date>`). Update this file directly
whenever your background or search focus changes — there's no review step,
it's just read fresh on the next sweep.

## Current role & level

Title, employer, how long, and two or three sentences on what you actually
do day to day. Specific enough that a reader who's never met you could tell
the difference between this and a similar-sounding title elsewhere.

## Stack & domains

Languages, frameworks, and tools you're fluent in — not everything you've
touched, what you'd list as strengths. Domains you've shipped real work in
(e.g. "real-time inference," "data pipelines at scale," "developer tooling"),
since that's what a `why` line should point at.

## What you're optimizing for in this search

Target level, a comp floor if you have one, remote/hybrid/onsite tolerance,
industries or company sizes you want or want to avoid. This is what turns a
generic fit judgment into one calibrated to *this* search — it should track
`data/criteria.yaml`, not contradict it.

## Notable work

A handful of concrete examples — specific projects, systems, outcomes — a
discovery session can cite directly in a `why` line. "Built a model registry
with staged rollouts and drift detection" beats "strong MLOps background."
